Key takeaways for your policy review

  1. Remote work comes with higher pay in the market. Fully remote developers earn about $12,000 (≈12%) more on average than hybrid developers, and the median remote developer earns about $12,800 more. If you reduce remote options, expect to compete against these higher remote pay levels when hiring and retaining talent.
  2. The gap holds up under every test we ran. A classic t-test, a version that allows for unequal spread (Welch), a 10,000-sample bootstrap, and a median-based test all point the same way. We are confident the gap is not a fluke of the sample.
  3. Education level is linked to pay in the US and Canada. Average salaries rise from $160,988 (Bachelor's) to $182,221 (Master's) to $197,701 (Professional). The differences across the three groups are statistically significant, though the gap between Master's and Professional degrees on its own is not clearly established.
  4. Satisfaction is fairly even across roles. Most job titles average 6 to 8 out of 10, so pay differences between roles are much larger than satisfaction differences.
  5. These are associations, not proof of cause. Remote workers in the survey are more concentrated in high-paying countries such as the US, and role and seniority also differ between groups. See More views for a country-by-country check.

Part 2 · Hybrid vs remote

Do remote developers earn more than hybrid developers?

Yes. We compared 7,803 hybrid and 6,933 remote developers using six complementary methods.

Discrepancies: none. The manual and built-in results match exactly. Assumptions: the variances are unequal (1.2059e+11 vs 3.4829e+10), so Welch's t-test is better. It gives a slightly smaller p-value but reaches the same conclusion.

What the p-value tells you

The p-value of 0.0098 means that if remote and hybrid developers truly earned the same on average, a gap this large would show up by chance less than 1% of the time. That is below the 5% threshold, so we reject the null hypothesis: there is statistically significant evidence of a difference, with remote workers earning more on average than hybrid workers.

Method notes

How to read these results

Mean vs median
The mean is the average; the median is the middle salary. Developer pay is pulled up by a few very high earners, so the median is closer to what a typical developer earns.
p-value
The probability of seeing a gap at least this large if there were truly no difference. Below 0.05 we call the result statistically significant.
95% confidence interval
A range that, with 95% confidence, contains the true difference. If it excludes $0, the difference is significant.
Bootstrap
Re-drawing the sample with replacement 10,000 times to see how much an average or median would vary from sample to sample.
Welch's t-test
A version of the t-test that does not assume both groups have the same spread. Used because hybrid salaries vary more than remote ones.
Mood's median test
Checks whether each group has a similar share of people above the overall median. Robust to extreme salaries.
ANOVA
Tests whether three or more group averages are all equal. A significant result means at least one differs.
